    Questions.

    Is this proof?

    No. An AI score is a signal, not proof — no detector can prove who wrote something. A scan certificate proves which exact text was scanned, when, and what score the app reported. It doesn’t prove the score is right. A draft trail is stronger evidence of your process, but it’s still something to weigh, not a verdict.

    Is my text stored?

    No. Files are read in your browser, the text is sent to the detector for scoring and isn’t kept or logged. When you stamp, only its fingerprint (a SHA-256 hash) goes on-chain — the text can’t be rebuilt from it.

    Why can it be wrong?

    Detectors look for patterns that are common in AI text: steady rhythm, stock phrases, predictable word choices. Careful, formal or non-native English writing often has the same patterns, and lightly edited AI text often doesn’t. Short texts give the detector very little to go on. That’s why Inkprint shows every signal and highlight, and a confidence band, not just a number.

    What goes on BOT Chain?

    For a scan certificate: the text’s fingerprint, the score, the confidence band, the detector’s name and the time. For writer’s proof: your piece’s title, and each draft’s fingerprint, word count and time. Never the text. Stamping needs a wallet and a small gas fee in BOT; scanning and verifying don’t.
